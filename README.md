# Scrum-Agile-Portfolio
CS 250 Software Development Lifecycle

This repository includes my SNHU Travel Sprint Review and Retrospective and my reflections on what I learned about software development during the course.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?
I interpret user needs by listening to feedback, asking questions and also making sure I understand what users want the program to do. User stories help explain who needs a particular feature, what they want and why it matters to them. In the SNHU Travel project changing the destination list into a Top 5 slideshow showed me how user needs could guide a specific feature of a program. Connecting user stories to acceptance criteria and test cases helped make it clear what the feature needed to do. This also showed me why asking for clarification is important before starting development. This way we are focused on the right things and we can deliver the best product. 

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
I approach developing programs by first understanding the requirements and then breaking the work into smaller tasks that I can build and test as I go. In future projects I want to use short Sprints, clear user stories and regular feedback to help manage changes throughout development. I also want to use Jira because I liked being able to see tasks organized as To Do, In Progress and Done. Sprint Retrospectives would help me look at what went well and decide what I could improve during the next Sprint.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
I think being a good team member means communicating clearly, following through on my responsibilities and being willing to help others. It also means asking questions when something is unclear and letting the team know about problems early before they become bigger problems. It’s very important to trust your team members and be transparent. This can lead to getting help if you are blocked. The wellness and detox changes in SNHU Travel showed me why developers, testers as well as the Product Owner need to understand the requirements in the same way. We all need to have clear expectations of what our role is. I would keep my tasks updated, listen to feedback and help work through problems so the team could keep moving toward our goal and deliver the best product.
